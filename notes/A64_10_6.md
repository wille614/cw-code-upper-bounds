# A(64,10,6) = 128

Let G = (Z/8Z)² and H = {0,4} × {0,4}. Use the two blocks

    B1 = {(0,0),(1,0),(2,1),(1,2),(6,2),(4,5)},
    B2 = {(0,0),(2,0),(0,1),(2,3),(1,5),(5,7)}.

The 60 ordered differences x−y, with distinct x,y in the same block,
are distinct and equal to G\H. This assertion requires only subtraction
modulo 8; the verifier checks the complete multiset. If two translates
share distinct points p,q, the difference p−q identifies a unique block
and a unique ordered pair in that block. Their translation parameters
are therefore equal. Thus the 128 translates are distinct and any two
meet in at most one point. Their characteristic vectors have weight 6
and distance at least 12−2 = 10.

The groups are the 16 cosets of H. No block contains two points in one
group; each pair in different groups occurs once. This is a 6-GDD of
type 4¹⁶, equivalently a GD[6,1,4;64], obtained by the standard relative
difference-family construction [1]. It uses the noncyclic group (Z/8Z)²;
the nonexistence of the corresponding pair of cyclic base blocks over
Z/64Z [3] does not exclude it.

For the upper bound, through any point the remaining five-element
parts of the incident blocks are disjoint. Each point occurs at most
floor(63/5) = 12 times. Counting incidences gives

    A(64,10,6) <= floor((64/6) floor(63/5)) = 128.

This is the Johnson bound [2]. Hence the lower and upper bounds agree.

The file `codes/a64.10.6.128` has one 64-character binary word per line.
Coordinate (x,y) is the character at position x+8y, counting from zero
at the left. `python verify.py 64 10 6` reconstructs all translates,
compares their set to the file, and checks all 8128 unordered word pairs.
The distance distribution is 4224 pairs at distance 10 and 3904 at distance 12.
