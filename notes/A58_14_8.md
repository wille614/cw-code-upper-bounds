# A(58,14,8) = 57

This is a consequence of known results. Interpret a code as eight-element
blocks on 58 points. Distance at least 14 means distinct blocks meet in
at most one point. At a fixed point the seven-element remainders of
incident blocks are disjoint, so its degree is at most floor(57/7) = 8.
If there are M blocks, incidence counting gives 8M <= 58·8, hence M <= 58.

Equality would force all 58 point degrees to be 8. The resulting incidence
structure is a symmetric configuration 58_8: it has 58 points and 58 lines,
eight incidences on each point and each line, and at most one common line
through any pair of points. Davydov et al. [1, Theorem 6.1], explicitly
citing Gropp [2, Theorem 2.4], exclude symmetric configurations
(k²−k+2)_k for 5 <= k <= 10. Since 8²−8+2 = 58, equality is impossible.
The upper bound is therefore 57. The cited theorem concerns all such
configurations, not merely cyclic ones.

For the lower bound, take the 57 lines of PG(2,7). There are 57 projective
points; each line contains 8 points and any two lines meet once. The
incidence vectors have length 57, weight 8 and mutual distance 14.
Appending a zero coordinate gives the required length 58 code.

`python verify.py 58 14 8` checks the projective-plane incidence calculation
over the prime field with seven elements and all numerical substitutions.
The configuration nonexistence theorem is an explicitly cited premise;
the program does not claim to reprove it.

2. H. Gropp, *Non-symmetric configurations with deficiencies 1 and 2*, in *Combinatorics ’90: Recent Trends and Applications*, Annals of Discrete Mathematics 52 (1992), 227–239. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0167-5060(08)70915-4). The theorem is used through its explicit statement in [1].
