# A(18,6,6) <= 183

The certificate combines the four-point constant-weight-code bound with
valid local inequalities, including 23 inequalities proved below. The
distance threshold d is a positive even integer. All polynomial identities
use X_u²=X_u and set products containing an incompatible pair to zero.
The prior premise |C|<=186 is independent of the claimed improvement:
Östergård's A(17,6,6)<=124 [5], followed by Johnson shortening on a zero
coordinate, gives floor(18·124/12)=186. The value 183 is never a premise.

Adding valid inequalities to coding bounds has early precedents in
Best–Brouwer [1] and Best et al. [2, Theorem 22]. The four-point framework
is Polak's [3]. Clique-packing leaves are classical objects, including
Brouwer's work on K₄ packings [6]. The specific inequalities and their
code-to-moment interpretation are given explicitly here.

## 1. From a code to moments

View a word as a w-subset of [n]. Its distance from another word is
2(w−|u intersection v|). Let X_u indicate membership in C, and
X_S=product_(u in S) X_u. Put V=binom(n,w). For a uniformly random
coordinate permutation g define z_C(S)=V E[X_S(gC)]. Then

    z_C(empty set)=V,       z_C({u})=|C|,       0<=z_C(S)<=V.

Variable zero in the model is the affine constant 1, not z(empty set).
A polynomial constant must consequently acquire a factor V.
For every polynomial p nonnegative on every code,
L_z(p)=sum_S p_S z(S)=V E[p(gC)]>=0. More generally, if h>=0 on every
code and f is a polynomial vector, L_z(hff^T) is positive semidefinite:
its quadratic form at a is V E[h(a^T f)²]>=0. Only entries with at most
four distinct word variables are retained. Positive scaling, fixed
congruences, summation and principal submatrices preserve this property.
This is the moment/localizing-matrix principle [7], here proved directly
for the finite code indicators.

## 2. The local packing

Use either the words or their complements and call their weight w'.
Put k=d/2+1 and ell=w'−k>=0. Choose disjoint coordinate sets R,Z of
sizes ell,b. Words containing R and avoiding Z give, after deleting R
and Z, k-subsets of an m=n−ell−b point set. They meet in at most one
point, since their original intersection is at most w'−d/2. Thus they
form a 2-(m,k,1) packing P. Complementation preserves distance and
uses the indicator of the original word; it does not replace X_u by 1−X_u.

Suppose (k−1)|(m−1) and binom(k,2)|binom(m,2). Set

    K=binom(m,2)/binom(k,2),  N=|P|,  t=K−N.

The leave L has the uncovered pairs as edges. If x is in r_x blocks,

    deg_L(x)=m−1−(k−1)r_x,       |E(L)|=binom(k,2)t.

Therefore t is a nonnegative integer and all degrees are nonnegative
multiples of k−1. All local evaluations below keep the same m-point
ambient set, including when the block family is replaced by a subset.

## 3. A one-block deficit and triangle inequalities

If t=0 the leave is empty. If t=1 its degree sum is k(k−1), so it has
at most k nonisolated vertices, each of degree at least k−1. Its
binom(k,2) edges require at least k vertices. Hence its nonisolated
part is exactly K_k.

Let T be its number of triangles and a=binom(k,3). Then

    F=T+a t(t−2)>=0.

At t=0 this is zero; at t=1, T=a makes it zero; at t>=2 both summands
are nonnegative. Thus NF, tF and H_(j,h)F are also nonnegative, where
H_(j,h) counts direction-words u with |u intersection R|=j and
|u intersection Z|=h.

Let I count unordered block pairs meeting in one point. Let Q count
unordered block triples with all pair intersections of size one and
empty triple intersection. Inclusion–exclusion on a triangle gives

    T=binom(m,3) − [(m−2)binom(k,2)−2a]N + (k−1)²I − Q.

Indeed the single-edge term is (m−2)binom(k,2)N; the two-edge term is
3aN+(k−1)²I; the three-edge term is aN+Q. Exactly two blocks cannot
cover the three edges: a block covering two edges contains the third,
which would violate pair uniqueness if another block covered it.
Consequently F has degree at most three, and the three kinds of
linear-count multiples have degree at most four. The square-free
coefficient f(S) is:

| Word set S | f(S) |
|---|---|
| Empty set | binom(m,3)+aK(K−2) |
| One block | a(3−2K)−[(m−2)binom(k,2)−2a] |
| Two blocks | 2a+(k−1)² 1[intersection size 1] |
| Three blocks | −1[all pair intersections size 1 and triple intersection empty] |
| At least four blocks | 0 |

The coefficient of NF is
g(S)=|S|f(S)+sum_(u in S) f(S\{u}), with g(empty set)=0.
The two terms account for multiplying by a word already present or a
new word. The coefficient of tF is Kf−g.

## 4. A leave with twelve edges

**Lemma.** A simple graph with twelve edges and all degrees divisible
by three has T+C₄>=4, where C₄ counts undirected, not necessarily
induced, four-cycles.

Proof. Delete isolated vertices and call the remaining number s. The
degree sum is 24, so s<=8. If s<=6 every positive degree can only be
3, giving sum at most 18. At s=7 the degree sequence is (6,3,3,3,3,3,3).
Removing the universal vertex leaves a 2-regular graph with six edges;
each of those edges makes a triangle with the removed vertex, so T>=6.

At s=8 the graph is cubic. Let c(u,v) count common neighbors. Counting
length-two paths, triangle edges, and four-cycle diagonals gives

    sum_(u<v) c(u,v)=24,
    sum_(uv an edge) c(u,v)=3T,
    sum_(u<v) binom(c(u,v),2)=2C₄.

There are sixteen nonedges. The integer inequality binom(c,2)>=c−1
therefore implies 2C₄>=8−3T. This proves the lemma at T=0 or T=1;
at T>=4 it is immediate.

Two intersecting triangles in a cubic graph must share an edge. If the
subgraph **induced by the union of their vertex sets** is K₄, it contains
four triangles. Otherwise it is a diamond. The diamond's two degree-three
vertices have no external neighbors; its other two vertices contribute
exactly two external edges. On the remaining four vertices the internal
degree sum is 12−2=10, so the induced graph has five edges and is another
diamond. Again there are at least four triangles. Consequently T<4
forces vertex-disjoint triangles, making T=3 impossible on eight vertices.

At T=2 call the triangles A,B and the other vertices x,y. An external
vertex cannot meet two vertices of the same triangle, because that gives
another triangle. If xy is absent, x has all three neighbors in A union B,
a contradiction. Thus xy is present; x and y each have one neighbor in
each triangle, with different neighbors within each triangle. The two
neighbors in A, together with x,y, give a four-cycle; B gives another.
Hence C₄>=2 and T+C₄>=4. All cases are covered.

The Wagner graph attains T=0,C₄=4. No assertion that every graph satisfying
this lemma is realizable as a particular packing leave is needed.

## 5. Two further polynomials

For k=4 the eligible m satisfy m=1 or 4 modulo 12, K=m(m−1)/12 and
|E(L)|=6t. Put S=T+C₄. At t=0, S=0; at t=1 the leave is K₄, so S=7;
at t=2 the lemma gives S>=4. Always S>=0. Thus

    H₂=S+5t²−12t>=0,
    H₄=24S+t(t−3)(t−5)(5t−26)>=0.

| t | Proved lower bound on S | 5t²−12t | t(t−3)(t−5)(5t−26)/24 |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 |
| 1 | 7 | −7 | −7 |
| 2 | 4 | −4 | −4 |
| 3 | 0 | 9 | 0 |
| 4 | 0 | 32 | 1 |
| 5 | 0 | 65 | 0 |
| Every integer t>=6 | 0 | positive | positive |

For a pair e, the uncovered-edge indicator is Y_e=1−sum_(u contains e)X_u.
It is zero or one on a packing. Triangle counts are sums of products of
three such indicators; four-cycle counts use four. Thus H₂,H₄ have
Boolean degree at most four. They are not further multiplied by a count.
For a compatible residual word set Q their coefficients are

    c(Q)=sum_(P subset Q) (−1)^(|Q|−|P|) H(P).

Every H(P) here is evaluated on the **same fixed m-point set**. This
is subset-lattice Möbius inversion: summing c(Q) over Q subset C gives
coefficient (1−1)^(|C\P|) on H(P), leaving only H(C). The degree bound
makes all coefficients above four vanish.

## 6. Averaging intervals and the anchored inequalities

For a nonempty word set Q, let I(Q)=|intersection Q| and U(Q)=|union Q|.
Exactly binom(I(Q),ell) binom(n−U(Q),b) intervals contain Q. The residual
word set in each interval is the same up to coordinate permutation.

For r=|Q|<=4 **distinct words**, let h_alpha be the number of coordinates
having binary column pattern alpha. Let s(h) be the number of
permutations in S_r preserving that histogram. Its coordinate orbit has
size

    o(Q)=n! / [(product_alpha h_alpha!) s(h)].

This is the orbit–stabilizer formula, accounting for same-pattern
coordinate permutations and word permutations. The variables contain
histograms of the r distinct rows, without repeated-row padding. For
each nonconstant term, multiply its residual coefficient by o(Q) and
the interval count. The constant is multiplied by
V binom(n,ell) binom(n−ell,b). A binomial coefficient with an invalid
lower argument means zero.

For H_(j,h)F exclude the class (j,h)=(ell,0), already represented by NF.
Choose an anchored word u from Q and put Q'=Q\{u}. If Q' is nonempty,
let A=intersection Q', B=[n]\union Q'. The interval count is

    binom(|A intersection u|,j) binom(|A\u|,ell−j)
      · binom(|B intersection u|,h) binom(|B\u|,b−h).

If Q' is empty it is

    binom(w',j) binom(n−w',ell−j)
      · binom(w'−j,h) binom(n−w'−ell+j,b−h).

Multiply by f(Q'), sum over u in Q and multiply by o(Q). A repeated
anchor could contribute only when u itself lies in the interval, which
means (j,h)=(ell,0); that excluded case is exactly NF. Dividing a whole
inequality by a positive integer gcd preserves its sign.

## 8. Validity of the other retained inequalities

The A₄ moment matrices have entries z(S union T), for D subset S and
|D|+2|S\D|<=4. On code indicators they are expectations of outer
products after extracting X_D, hence positive semidefinite. The
empty-anchor and two-word-anchor matrices, together with nonnegative
moments, have the interpretation in [3]. Fixed symmetry subspaces and
principal submatrices preserve positivity. This is the complete A₄
framework, rather than identifying every four-word condition with B₄.

The other families have the following direct justifications. All products
are Booleanized and only entries of degree at most four are retained.

| Family | Code-validity proof |
|---|---|
| Anticode vacancy, scalar and matrix | Put q=w−d/2+1. For a set R of size q+2r, the family Q={u:|u intersection R|>=q+r} is q-intersecting. A code contains at most one of its words. Thus h_Q=1−sum_(u in Q)X_u>=0. Use X_D h_Q with |D|<=3, or X_a h_Q ff^T with constant/linear components f and retained degree <=4. The intersection assertion follows from 2(q+r)−(q+2r)=q. |
| Matching occupancy | If a residual interval has min(k,m−k)=d/2, its words or complements are pairwise disjoint. At 3d/2<=m<2d its occupancy N<=3. For j=0,1,2,3, P_j=sum_(r=j)^3 (−1)^(r−j) binom(r,j) binom(N,r)=1[N=j]. Thus X_a P_j>=0, with degree <=4. |
| Integer occupancy polynomials | The shortening bound J(m,d,k) is 1 when min(k,m−k)<d/2; otherwise it is at most both floor(m J(m−1,d,k−1)/k) and floor(m J(m−1,d,k)/(m−k)), after complementing if necessary and treating weight zero as size one. Each recursion follows by counting ones or zeros and induction. If p has degree <=4 and p(i)>=0 for every integer 0<=i<=J, then p(N)>=0. Finite differences express it in moments of degree <=4. |
| Occupancy matrices | For an **integer** j, (N−j)(N−j−1)>=0, and N(J−N)>=0. Multiply by ff^T where components of f are constant or linear coordinate-class counts; retain only degree <=4. Apply §1. |
| Cardinality inequalities, scalar and matrix | M=sum_u X_u<=186 gives 186−M>=0, X_D(186−M)>=0 for |D|<=3, and X_a(186−M)ff^T positive in moments for constant/linear f. Set union handles repeated words; retained entries have degree <=4. |

These are proofs of validity, not assertions that every retained family
is necessary or independent of the others. The numerical dual check uses
the supplied integer coefficients; the 23 additional rows also receive
the independent semantic coefficient check described in §7. It does not
rederive every symmetry-reduced coefficient of the inherited A₄ matrices.

## 9. Exact certificate and conclusion

Write a model block as F_b(z)=F_b,0+sum_i z_i F_b,i. The certificate
supplies rational positive-semidefinite matrices Y_b. With off-diagonal
trace entries counted twice, define

    c_0=sum_b <F_b,0,Y_b>,
    r_i=1[i=1]+sum_b <F_b,i,Y_b>.

Since sum_b <F_b(z),Y_b>=c_0−z_1+sum_i r_i z_i>=0 and
0<=z_i<=V=18564, one obtains

    |C|=z_1 <= c_0+18564 sum_i max(r_i,0).

| Quantity | Exact value |
|---|---:|
| c_0 | 18397846380536651084584137185081/100000000000000000000000000000 |
| Positive-residual compensation | 1982087714623226562329523/5000000000000000000000000000 |
| Sum U | 18397886022290943549115383775541/100000000000000000000000000000 |
| Decimal U | 183.97886022290943549115383775541 |
| floor(U) | 183 |

The model has 941 nonconstant variables and 16387 dual blocks, largest
order 47. `python verify.py 18 6 6` checks the public model/certificate
binding, reconstructs the 23 rows, and verifies every rational matrix
by exact fraction-free elimination. A negative pivot or a zero pivot
with a nonzero remaining row is rejected; every exact division is checked.
No supplied factorization is taken on trust. It then recalculates every
residual and all entries in the table. Because |C| is an integer and
U<184, the claimed upper bound follows. Residual compensation is a
standard exact-certification device; see [4].
